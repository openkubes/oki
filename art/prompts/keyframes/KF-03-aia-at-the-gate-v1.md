# KF-03 - Aia at the gate - candidate 01

Create one finished landscape children’s-book illustration, approximately 3:2, as a single cinematic scene without panels.

Use both attached canonical references exactly: Aia’s lavender rabbit-like explorer identity with long expressive ears, violet field outfit and visibly overfilled backpack of rolled plans; and the four canonical sensor companions—Scout teal with binoculars, Meter amber with a rectangular analog measurement gauge, Check blue with a clipboard, Proof green holding a large green check mark. Preserve their silhouettes, colors, proportions and instruments. This is story artwork, not a character sheet.

Scene: a large, beautiful change gate stands across a path. In the foreground before the closed or just-beginning-to-open gate are Aia, a rolled proposal and the four sensors. Aia has brought many ideas: plans peek from the overfilled backpack, and one improved blueprint lies open for review. Aia is helpful and engaged, pointing at the plan, but clearly does not command the gate. Scout looks through binoculars, Meter reads the rectangular gauge, Check compares the clipboard with the plan, and Proof presents the large green check as an evidence summary.

At the gate stands one kind adult human builder. The human holds the reviewed blueprint in one hand and the scene’s only unmistakable brass key in the other, at the gate lock. The human is the accountable decision maker. Behind the gate, a real path change or new bridge segment glows softly, showing that actual change lies beyond approval. The gate may be opening a little because the human is turning the key after review.

Critical governance truth: Aia has absolutely no key—not in a hand, belt, backpack, badge or accessory. The sensors have no key and do not open the gate. Proof’s green check is large and clearly shaped only as a check mark; it is visually separate from the lock and cannot be mistaken for a key. Aia helps think, sensors check facts, the human decides.

Composition: strong left-to-right narrative flow from proposal and evidence, to human and lock, to real change beyond the gate. Keep all six story participants readable: Aia, four distinct sensors, one human builder. Aia and sensors occupy the “before” side; only the human touches the lock. Emotion: trust, collaboration, thoughtful responsibility—not suspicion or confrontation.

Visual style: premium hand-painted European children’s-book illustration; soft watercolor and gouache on subtly textured warm paper; delicate pencil-and-ink contours; luminous golden-hour light; magical but grounded; expressive faces; rich enough for a full-page spread; never glossy 3D, photorealistic, vector-flat or a corporate workflow diagram. Preserve Aia’s violet/lilac palette, each sensor’s canonical color, and reserve green for Proof’s passed evidence.

Do not include any words, labels, letters, UI, logos, trademarks, watermark or decorative border. Do not add Oki. Do not add a second human, a second key, repair tools, weapons, crowns or command symbols.
