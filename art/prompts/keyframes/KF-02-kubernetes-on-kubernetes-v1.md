# KF-02 - Kubernetes on Kubernetes - candidate 01

Create one finished landscape children’s-book illustration, approximately 3:2, with a single coherent scene and no panel grid.

Use the attached canonical Oki character sheet as the exact identity reference. Preserve Oki’s teal island-spirit face, leafy hair, warm eyes, ochre-yellow work jacket, teal shorts, brown boots and work satchel. This is story artwork, not a character sheet.

Scene: a joyful visual revelation of “an island inside an island.” Show one large host island as the dominant ground. The host island has exactly one clearly readable town hall. Within a prepared workshop area on that same ground sits a smaller, unmistakably independent guest island. The guest island has its own continuous shoreline or glowing boundary, its own paths, and exactly one separate town hall. The two cluster identities must be immediately understandable even to a child: shared physical ground, separate island, separate town hall. Suggest beneath the guest island—through a gentle storybook cutaway in the workshop floor—several plain virtual building places and connecting lines. Those building places are infrastructure only and must not have town halls.

Oki is the personified host island and reacts with delighted, thoughtful surprise near the boundary of the smaller island. Compose him as visually rooted in the outer island—his teal coloring and nearby vegetation merge into the host terrain—so he does not read as an unrelated visitor or resident. He is walking once around the guest island and wondering how many islands can fit inside an island.

Architecture truth: exactly two Kubernetes clusters are visible and therefore exactly two town halls in total—one for the host island and one for the guest island. The small guest is a complete cluster, not merely a VM. The workshop/virtual building places are not clusters. Do not show a cluster egg in this acceptance keyframe; it is the moment after birth.

Composition: clear nested spatial hierarchy, slightly elevated three-quarter view. The outer island fills most of the frame; the inner island is large enough to read as independent, with water, luminous boundary or a shallow moat separating it from the workshop ground. Keep both town halls visually distinct and easy to count. Emotion: playful discovery and conceptual wonder.

Visual style: premium hand-painted European children’s-book illustration; soft watercolor and gouache on warm textured paper; gentle pencil-and-ink linework; cheerful daylight; tactile natural materials; detailed but uncluttered; never glossy 3D, photorealistic, technical-diagram-like or vector-flat. Use Oki’s teal/ochre palette, natural greens for the host, and a calm blue accent for the guest island.

Do not include words, labels, letters, UI, arrows, logos, trademarks, watermark or decorative border. No Aia, no sensors, no human authority scene. No more than two town halls. No town hall on a VM or building place.
